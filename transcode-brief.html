For project management

Two separate causes, one already fixed

The source files. Resolved The masters were encoded beyond what the headset hardware can decode, so playback silently fell back to a degraded path. Re-encoded to a new standard, verified on an actual Quest 3S, confirmed excellent.

The platform's import transcode. Needs dev work Every upload is re-encoded into a ladder that appears to top out at 4K H.264. Because a 360° video wraps the entire frame around the viewer's head, a 4K rung shows about half the detail the headset can display.

The ask is either a “skip transcode” option for already-optimised VR files — the smaller change — or raising the ladder's top rung for VR content. Neither requires new infrastructure, and a reference file encoded to the proposed spec is ready for testing.

This is not a quality-versus-cost trade. It improves image quality and cuts storage and bandwidth at the same time, and the saving scales across the whole library including the newly purchased VR Gorilla titles. The risk of deferring is that every VR title added inherits the same ceiling — a library growing at a quality level below what the hardware can show, at roughly nine times the necessary storage cost.

The core point

Why 4K is not enough for VR

This is the thing that makes VR different from flat video, and it is easy to miss when reusing a standard ABR ladder built for flat content.

A flat 4K video puts 3840 pixels across the screen. A 360° video spreads those same 3840 pixels around the viewer's entire head — but the headset only shows about 96° at a time. The viewer sees roughly a quarter of the frame, stretched across the full display.

So the meaningful unit is pixels per degree, not resolution.

The headset displays 1832 pixels per eye across its ~96° field of view. A 4K 360° frame can only supply 1024 of them — the display is asked to show detail that was never encoded.

Two findings that should shape the ladder

AV1 beat HEVC by 1.2 dB while using 22% less bandwidth. Quest 3S has AV1 hardware decode (c2.qti.av1.decoder, confirmed on-device). H.264 is the worst choice at these frame sizes.

At a fixed bitrate, higher resolution made quality worse. The 8K encode scored lowest of all seven — spread across 59 megapixels at 45 Mbps, every pixel is starved. The fix is not simply “encode bigger”; 5760×5760 is the measured optimum for this budget.

For context, the original master was itself out of spec: 7680×7680 H.264 at 251 Mbps against the 220 Mbps limit, with a nonstandard level (level_idc=100; the H.264 standard stops at 6.2). That has been fixed on our side.

Requested changes — in priority order

What we are asking for

  1. Add a passthrough option

    A per-item “already optimised / skip transcode” flag. The cheapest fix and correct in principle: pre-conformed VR masters should not be re-encoded. Re-compressing an already compressed delivery file costs a generation of quality for no benefit.

  2. If the ladder stays, raise the top rung for VR content

    Rung 1 — 5760×5760 AV1 at 34 Mbps (primary, matches headset capability). Rung 2 — 4608×4608 AV1 at 15 Mbps. Rung 3 — 3840×3840 AV1 at 8 Mbps (weak-network fallback). Keep top-bottom stereo packing and equirectangular projection unchanged throughout.

  3. Do not transcode VR content to H.264

    Far less efficient at these frame sizes, and it is what caused the original problem.

  4. Confirm the transcoder can decode AV1 input

    Many pipelines decode H.264 and HEVC reliably but not AV1. If it cannot, tell us and we will supply a high-bitrate HEVC mezzanine for import instead — but we need to know rather than guess.

Implementation detail that silently breaks VR playback

FFmpeg discards 360/stereo metadata on every output, including -c copy. If the transcode pipeline uses FFmpeg and does not explicitly re-inject it, output files lose the sv3d/st3d boxes and the older Google Spherical V1 uuid XML. Players then fall back to showing a flat, doubled image instead of a sphere.

Serene appears to carry projection in CMS fields (Video Angle, Content Shape, 3D Layout), which may mask this — but any file leaving the platform for another player will be broken. We have a working injector (~200 lines, no dependencies) that writes both metadata generations and can be shared.
